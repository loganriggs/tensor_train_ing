## Patterns
1. **PID-chained queues are expensive to reorder.** Each `chain.sh` waited on the previous one's PID, so changing priority meant verifying
   4 process trees and killing them last-first to avoid a premature start. Fixed: `scripts/run_queue.sh` puts the whole queue in one
   process with an ordered entry list. Reordering now means killing one waiting process and relaunching it with a new list (safe while it is
   still in its wait loop).
2. **Single-seed conclusion, again, and in the opposite direction.** "Width scaling no longer reduces the high-LR penalty at 4× width" was
   written from seed 101. Seed 202 showed something stronger: the scaled arm *diverges* on some seeds. The 08:50 rule (single-seed
   results are preliminary) held, because the section was labelled per-seed, so no claim had to be withdrawn. But the remedy (QK-clip) sat 4th
   in the queue behind lower-stakes work. **Rule: when a result raises a stability or correctness concern, its remedy experiment goes
   first in the queue.**
3. **`git add -f` on a mixed path list** forced in a gitignored file. Rule: never `-f` with more than the one intended path.
